# A change to the derive route is judged by the phases

The derive route turns an owner's answers into a design. What it does is decided by its doctrine,
its ledger schema, its catalog and its building blocks. A change to any of them changes every design
the route derives, for every domain at once.

Today a route change runs as its own dossier. The dossier holds a short note saying what changed and
why. A seal records the route as the change left it, and re-derives every ledger. A derivation under
any other route is refused. Nothing judges the note. A route change is held to less than a change to
one business domain, although it reaches every domain.

The lifecycle already says how a change whose deliverable is authored, not constructed, is governed.
It runs the phases from the seed to governance intent, and it is complete there. The route's
deliverable is authored: its rules and its code are written by a person, and construction renders
none of them. So a route change should run the same phases.

We want a route change to be sealed only when its dossier carries the seed, the change request, the
domain model, the analysis, the business model, the business intent and the governance intent. Each
must be admissible against the documents before it, by the rule sets sealed in the composition the
dossier pins. The seal should record each verdict and the rule set that gave it.

Seals recorded before this change carry a note and no verdicts. They are history, and they stay as
they are. Once one seal carries verdicts, every later seal must carry them too.

This change itself is a route change. It is the first one judged this way.

Out of scope: judging a route change past governance intent, because nothing in it is constructed;
re-judging a sealed dossier against a later composition; and changing what the derive route derives.
